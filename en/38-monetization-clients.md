# How to find your first clients through people you know

**Time:** about 25 min reading + 40 min practice

---

## The gist

The most expensive client is a cold one. The cheapest is someone who already knows you. A Trust Map is a map of your warm contacts. A warm conversation is how you turn them into your first paying clients without running a single ad.

## Key concepts

- Your first 3–5 projects are paid practice, not a full-scale business
- Trust Map: 20 contacts as your starting base
- Warm conversations: research, don't sell
- Referrals: happy clients are usually willing to recommend you if you ask, but most sellers never ask
- Your portfolio (your collection of finished work) gets built from your first projects

---

## Theory

### The biggest beginner mistake

"First I'll build the perfect product, then I'll go look for clients."

🎨 **Picture this:** a chef opens a restaurant and spends a year working on the menu in an empty dining room, without knowing whether anyone would even come. It should be the other way around: first you sit five friends down, serve them soup, hear "needs more salt," and only then put together a 50-item menu.

This is a trap. While you polish a product in a vacuum:
- You don't know your clients' real pain points
- You automate things that may not be needed at all
- You spend months on guesses instead of testing them against reality

**The right order:**
1. Talk to 5–10 potential clients
2. Find out their real pain points
3. Build the smallest solution for exactly that
4. Get your first payment
5. Improve it based on how it's actually used

---

### First projects come with different expectations

🎨 **Picture this:** your first client is like the first pancake off the griddle. What matters isn't that it came out a little lopsided, but that it taught you how to pour the batter.

Your first 3–5 projects are **paid practice.** Optimize for experience and testimonials, not for maximum income.

What a first project gives you:
- You learn how real clients describe their problems (not the way you expected)
- You see where the agent works well and where it gets stuck
- You get your first testimonial to show the next client
- You build confidence

A lower price on your first projects isn't about being "cheap." It's a deliberate choice: as much experience as possible with as little risk of hearing "no." The right range depends on your niche, your area and your market; how to work out your own floor and ceiling is in the lesson [How to set a price](d02-pricing-simple.md). Any numbers in this lesson are a made-up example, not a promise of income.

### Trust Map: a map of your warm contacts

🎨 **Picture this:** your Trust Map is your Rolodex (that old spinning file of business cards). Every business owner has one, but few people go through it on purpose. Go through yours, and you'll find 3–5 people who already trust you and who have exactly the problem you know how to fix.

A Trust Map is a simple table of everyone you know who could:
- Become a client themselves
- Recommend you to someone else

**How to build your Trust Map:**

Take a sheet of paper or a spreadsheet and write down everyone you know:
- Friends and relatives who run a business
- Former coworkers and employers
- People you've met at networking events
- People you've emailed about work over the last 2 years
- People on LinkedIn you know personally

**Table format (a Trust Map template):**

| Name | How I know them | Business / role | Possible pain point | Priority (1–3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sarah K. | A local business owners' Facebook group | Owns an online store | Processing orders by hand, answering the same questions over and over | 1 (high) |
| Mike R. | Former coworker | Real estate agent | Lots of routine back-and-forth with clients, preparing paperwork | 2 (medium) |
| Maria T. | LinkedIn | HR manager at a tech company | Going through 200+ résumés a week, screening candidates | 2 (medium) |
| James S. | Friend of a friend | Owns a dental practice | Booking patients by hand, appointment reminders | 1 (high) |
| Lisa L. | Networking event | Content manager | Posting to 5 social networks every day, repetitive copy | 2 (medium) |

Goal: **20+ contacts.** That doesn't mean they'll all become clients. It means you'll have people to talk to.

**Where to find contacts:**
- Local Facebook groups, Nextdoor and other communities where you're active
- LinkedIn: your connections with titles like "owner," "CEO," "director" or "founder"
- Your phone's contacts: scroll through the names
- Email: search your inbox for "hi" or "thanks" over the last 2 years, and you'll see everyone who has written to you

### Warm conversations: a conversation that doesn't sell

🎨 **Picture this:** a doctor doesn't walk in and say "buy this medicine." They ask where it hurts, how long it's been going on, what you've tried. Only after listening do they make a diagnosis and suggest treatment. A warm conversation is a doctor's appointment, not a pharmacy counter.

A warm conversation is a meeting or a call where your goal is to **learn**, not to **sell.**

Why it works:
- People like giving advice and talking about their problems
- A conversation without sales pressure is pleasant
- You get real information, not answers people give "just to get rid of you"
- If you find their problem, they'll ask you themselves: "Could you help with that?"

**How to start the conversation:**

You can send this as a text, an email or a LinkedIn message:

```
"Hi [name]! I'm doing some research right now on where business owners
lose the most time to routine work. Could we talk for 15-20 minutes?
I'd love to hear your take."
```

Notice: no "sell," no "I have a solution," no "I'd like to offer you something."

**Key questions for the conversation:**

Question 1, an open start:
"Tell me, what does a typical work week look like for you?"

Question 2, finding the pain:
"What repetitive or tedious task eats up the most of your time?"

Question 3, how deep the pain goes:
"Roughly how many hours a week does that take?"

Question 4, what the problem costs:
"If someone did this for you, how much would that cost, or how much would you save?"

Question 5, what they've already tried:
"Have you tried doing anything about it? What didn't work?"

**What you're looking for:** a task that takes 5+ hours a week, comes up regularly and doesn't need an expert's judgment, just someone to do it.

### The pattern: from conversation to project

🎨 **Picture this:** finding the pattern is like tuning an old radio. After the first few conversations, it's mostly static. By the tenth, the same tune keeps coming through clearly, from different people. That's your product.

After 5–10 conversations you'll see a pattern: 2–3 problems that come up again and again. That's the signal: here's your first product.

Example: in 7 out of 10 conversations, business owners mention "I spend a lot of time answering the same questions by email." That's your way in.

The next step:

```
"Hey, you mentioned you spend 3 hours a day answering emails.
I happen to have a tool that automates exactly that.
Want me to show you how it would work in your case?"
```

The transition feels natural: you already know the problem, and you're offering a specific solution.

### Referrals: the resource nobody uses

🎨 **Picture this:** you built your neighbor a deck, and they love it. Not asking for a referral is like walking away without leaving your card. And your neighbor knows three other neighbors who want a deck too. One question = three potential clients.

In practice, it goes like this:
- most happy clients are willing to give a referral if you ask
- most sellers never ask

That means you lose potential new clients simply because you don't ask.

**When and how to ask:**

After a project wraps up successfully, or after a good conversation:

```
"[Name], I'm glad this helped. Is there anyone in your network,
other business owners or managers, who might find this useful too?
I'd be happy to talk with them."
```

Important: don't ask right at the start of the work. Only after the person has gotten real value.

**A referral program for later:** once you have 5+ clients, you can offer a formal referral program: "Refer a client, get 10% of their first project." But at the start, a simple question works better than any program.

---

### Cold outreach: only when you're out of warm contacts

🎨 **Picture this:** warm contacts are like fishing in a pond you know, where you've already seen fish. Cold outreach is casting a net into a lake you've never seen. Both can work, but the second takes far more tries.

Cold outreach (writing to people who don't know you) is much harder and converts poorly: a few percent reply, and a fraction of a percent become clients (the exact numbers depend on the niche; we don't have precise statistics).

If you've worked through your warm contacts and want to try cold outreach:

**Rules:**
1. Personalization is a must. "I see you sell X through Shopify and have 500+ SKUs. Does managing the catalog by hand take a lot of time?"
2. Keep it short. Three sentences max in the first message.
3. End with one specific question, not an offer.
4. Don't sell in the first contact. Ask and learn.

But start with your warm contacts. The 20 people on your Trust Map are likely to bring you more first clients than 200 cold emails.

## Practice

**Exercise:** build your Trust Map and schedule conversations

1. Create a table (Google Sheets, Notion or just a text file)

2. Fill in at least 20 rows. Don't filter yet; write everyone down:
   ```
   Name | How you know them | What they do | Possible pain point | Priority (1-3)
   ```

3. Mark your top 5 with priority 1 (high)

4. Write a personalized message for each of your top 5:
   ```
   Hi [name]! I'm doing some research right now...
   ```

5. Put it on your calendar: three conversations next week

6. After each conversation: add the key pain points to the table

7. After 5 conversations: find the pattern that keeps repeating. That's your first product

## Common mistakes

**1. Waiting too long before the first conversation**
"First I'll finish the course, then I'll polish my portfolio, then..." That's procrastination in disguise. Message 5 people this week. You don't need to be 100% ready.

**2. Pricing too low**
A price far below the market isn't "an affordable price to win clients." It signals "I'm not sure my work is any good." Work out your price with the lesson [How to set a price](d02-pricing-simple.md) and raise it with every new project.

**3. Selling in the first message**
```
❌ "Hi! I do AI automation, want to hear about it?"
✅ "Hi! I'm researching where business owners lose time to routine work. Could we talk for 15 minutes?"
```

**4. Not writing down what you heard**
After 10 conversations, you won't remember what the first person said. Write down the key pain points RIGHT AFTER each conversation.

**5. Not asking for referrals**
Most happy clients will refer you if you ask. Just ask after a successful project.

## Template: a warm conversation script (5 key questions)

Use this script as the structure for a 15–20 minute conversation:

```
OPENING (2 minutes):
"Hi [name]! Thanks for making the time. I'm researching
where business owners lose the most time to routine work.
I'd really value your perspective."

QUESTION 1: The big picture
"Tell me, what does a typical work week look like for you?"
→ Listen. Take notes. Don't interrupt.

QUESTION 2: Finding the pain
"What repetitive or tedious task eats up the most
of your time?"
→ Look for: a task that repeats and doesn't need expertise.

QUESTION 3: How deep the pain goes
"Roughly how many hours a week does that take?"
→ Under 2 hours: a weak pain point. 5+ hours: a strong one.

QUESTION 4: What the problem costs
"If someone did this for you, how much would you
be willing to pay?"
→ This gives you an anchor for your pricing.

QUESTION 5: What they've already tried
"Have you tried doing anything about it? What didn't work?"
→ You learn what they've tried and why it didn't help.

WRAP-UP (2 minutes):
"Thank you! This is really helpful. If I find a solution
that could help, would it be OK to show it to you?"
→ You get permission to follow up.
```

## Key takeaways
> Your first client is close by: in your phone's contacts, on LinkedIn or in a local Facebook group you're part of. You don't need ads to find your first 3–5 clients.
> Research, don't sell. Someone who feels understood (instead of sold to) will ask on their own: "Could you help with that?"
> Most happy clients will give you a referral if you ask. Always ask after a successful project.
> Results depend on your niche, your market and your work: this lesson gives you a method, not a guarantee.
