# Cold outreach that gets replies: email, LinkedIn, video

**Time:** about 30 min reading + 45 min practice

The numbers in this lesson (reply rates, volumes, tool prices) are rough guides for doing the math, not statistics and not a promise of results. Measure your own. For current tool prices, check each tool's website and the [Tools](https://aimayak.com/tools/) section.

---

## The gist

You have a product. Your landing page works. But your inbox is empty and nobody is booking time on your Calendly. It's a familiar spot for a lot of indie founders: the product is ready, and the customers aren't there.

Cold outreach feels scary because we remember the 1990s: telemarketers calling at dinnertime, junk mail stuffing the mailbox, "Buy our insurance right now!" That doesn't work in 2026, and it shouldn't.

Modern cold outreach is a **tactful introduction between two people who don't know each other yet**. You don't write "buy from me." You write "I think I understand your problem, I have a solution for it, would you like to take a look?" It isn't knocking on doors. It's a note that lands on the right desk on the right day.

A good cold message reads as if a sharp acquaintance happened to understand exactly what's bugging you. A bad one reads as if a bot spat it out through a mail merge.

🎨 **Picture this:** the difference between a guest who shows up uninvited and yells "Buy!" and someone who knocks, introduces themselves, explains why they stopped by and offers to grab you a coffee. The second person gets the door opened a lot more often.

---

## 🎯 The main rule: outcomes, not features

Most cold messages are bad because they sell **what you do** instead of **what the other person gets**.

❌ "I have a SaaS product that automates reports with AI analytics and a Slack integration."
✅ "Most finance teams spend Friday pulling the weekly report together by hand. My clients got that Friday back."

The first one is about you. The second is about them. The person reading your message should think, "Oh, that's exactly my situation," not "Oh, another SaaS pitch."

## Key concepts

- **ICP (ideal customer profile)**: a precise picture of the person you sell to: job title, company size, industry and a specific pain
- **Reply rate**: the share of messages you send that get a reply. The sharper your ICP and research, the higher it goes. We don't have reliable market benchmarks, so measure your own
- **Sequence**: a series of 3-4 follow-up messages after the first touch
- **Warmup**: gradually warming up an email domain before you send at volume (otherwise you end up in spam)
- **Personalization signal**: a specific fact about the recipient that proves you're not sending in bulk
- **CTA (call to action)**: the one specific ask at the end of the message, usually "15-minute call?"
- **Multi-touch**: a series of touches across different channels (email → LinkedIn → X, formerly Twitter)
- **Loom DM**: a short personal video message (15-30 seconds) where you share your screen and walk through their website

| Channel | Reply rate (rough) | Effort per contact | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold email** | below average | 5 min (with a template) | B2B prospects, at scale |
| **LinkedIn DM** | average | 7 min | B2B executives, senior people |
| **X (Twitter) DM** | average | 5 min | Founders, indie hackers |
| **Cold call** | low | 10 min | High-ticket B2B |
| **Loom video DM** | above average | 15 min | Your top 20 high-value prospects |

🎨 **Picture this:** channels are like fishing gear. Cold email is a net (lots of volume, an average catch). LinkedIn is a rod with good bait (slower, but the fish are bigger). Loom is a spear (one throw, but right on target).

**Which one to pick:**
- B2B SaaS with a small average deal (ACV, annual contract value: what one customer pays per year): cold email first, LinkedIn second
- B2B SaaS with a high ACV: LinkedIn first, Loom for your top 20
- An indie product with an inexpensive subscription: X DMs + indie hacker communities
- Enterprise, with a very high ACV: cold calls + LinkedIn + Loom (multi-touch)

## Anatomy of an effective cold message: 5 parts

Every message that works has five parts. Drop one and your reply rate drops.

```
[1. Hook: a relevance signal]
   "I noticed your team just launched [thing]..."

[2. Pain: a specific problem]
   "Most [your audience] I talk to say [pain]..."

[3. Solution: the value]
   "I built [thing] that [outcome] for [niche]..."

[4. Proof: credibility]
   "It's already working for [example client/case]: [metric]..."

[5. Ask: a soft one]
   "If it's of interest, could I show you in 15 minutes?"
```

**Length by channel:**
- Email: 50-100 words (aim short)
- LinkedIn: 150-200 words
- X (Twitter) DM: 30-50 words
- Loom voiceover: 60-90 seconds

🎨 **Picture this:** the structure is a five-act play. The hook is the curtain going up. The pain is the conflict. The solution is the hero. The proof is the witnesses. The ask is the finale, with an invitation to come back for an encore. Skip an act and the audience walks out.

## Ready-made templates: copy, paste and adapt

### Cold email template (B2B SaaS)

```
Subject: [Their pain, in their words] at [Their company]?

Hi [First Name],

I saw that [Their company] [specific recent achievement / launch / news].
Congrats.

I work with [3 similar companies in their space] on [specific
problem]. Many teams spend [N] hours a week on [process],
while [solution] gets it done in [M] hours. [Use only real numbers
from your own client work.]

For [Their company], that could mean about $X/month saved, or Y hours
freed up for [strategic work].

If it's useful, I'd be glad to show you a 15-minute live demo.
Would that be worth a look?

Best,
[Your name]
[Title], [Your company]
[Calendly link]
[Your company's mailing address]
Not the right fit? Just reply "no thanks" and I won't follow up.
```

**Why it works:**
- The subject line is a question about their pain, not about your product
- The first line is about **them** (the hook), not about you
- Specific numbers from your real client work instead of a vague "many hours" (never make them up)
- The proof comes through "I work with 3 similar companies": quiet social proof, and it has to be true
- The ask is soft: "15 minutes," not "a 45-minute demo session"
- The footer has your mailing address and an easy way to say no, the basics that US anti-spam rules expect (see "Legal basics" below)

### LinkedIn DM template

```
Hi [Name],

I saw your post about [their recent post topic]. Your point about [insight]
was spot on.

A question for you: how does your team handle [specific process] today?
I work with [niche] on [solution], and I often hear that [pain].

No pitch here. I'm genuinely curious how you do it.

[Optional: a small value-add right away, like a link to a relevant article or a free tool]
```

**Why it works:**
- The first line is a compliment built on **specifics** from their post (it proves you actually read it)
- "No pitch here" takes the pressure off and lowers the barrier, as long as it's true. If you plan to pitch later, don't pretend you weren't selling
- A value-add with no strings attached: you give before you ask

### X (Twitter) DM template

```
Hey [name], your thread on [topic] was spot on.

Quick question: [specific question about their work]

No need for a long reply, just curious. I'm building [niche solution] myself.
```

**Why it works:**
- Short and casual, which fits the culture on X
- "Quick question" signals you're not asking for an essay
- "Just curious" makes it easy to reply
- At the end you mention that you're **building something yourself**: you come across as a peer, not a salesperson

### Loom video DM (for high-value prospects)

This is your "spear": 5-10 a week, aimed at your most valuable prospects.

**Structure (90 seconds max):**
1. **0-10 sec:** A personal intro, face on camera. "Hi Alex, [your name] here from [company]."
2. **10-30 sec:** Open their website and share your screen. "I noticed you're doing X..."
3. **30-60 sec:** Point out 2-3 specific things on their site (this proves it isn't a bulk message)
4. **60-80 sec:** Connect their situation to your solution. "We work with similar companies, and..."
5. **80-90 sec:** A soft CTA. "If this is interesting, happy to show you a 10-minute demo. My Calendly link is below."

**Tool:** Loom https://www.loom.com has a free plan with limits (see the site)

**Tip:** include the video's preview thumbnail in your first message; it makes a big difference to the click-through rate (CTR). Loom generates the thumbnail automatically.

## Volume math: be realistic

Most people fail at cold outreach because they send 10 messages, get no replies and decide it "doesn't work." Cold outreach is a numbers game AND a quality game.

**Goal (an illustrative example):** 5 paying clients

**A sample funnel calculation** (the numbers are made up to show the method, not a forecast; plug in your own conversion rates):
- Send **500 messages** → 50 replies (10%)
- 50 replies → 20 take a call (40%)
- 20 calls → 5 close (25%)
- = **5 clients**

**The math on time:**
- 50 messages a week = 10 weeks to send 500
- 100 a week = 5 weeks
- 200 a week = 2.5 weeks (but quality drops, and so does your reply rate)

Start with a volume you can keep up while still doing real research: that's a few hours a day of focused work. Only your own tracking will show your real conversion rates.

🎨 **Picture this:** cold outreach is like working out at the gym. One trip to the gym doesn't build muscle. Three times a week for 8 weeks does. Consistency matters more than intensity.

---

## Personalization vs. volume: the big tradeoff

| Approach | Time per message | Reply rate (rough) | Volume per week |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mass spam** | 30 sec | close to zero | lots of messages, almost no replies |
| **Template + first name** | 2 min | low | medium volume |
| **Researched** (company → person) | 5-7 min | noticeably higher | dozens of messages |
| **Highly personalized + Loom** | 15-20 min | the highest | a handful to a few dozen messages |

**Recommendation:** the researched approach (option 3) usually gives you the best return on your time. Volume and quality stay in balance. Check it against your own numbers.

**The stack approach:**
- Most of your time → researched outreach (dozens of messages a week)
- A smaller share of your time → Loom for your top 5-10 prospects a week
- 0% of your time → mass spam (never do it: it damages your domain and your reputation)

## Sequences: multi-touch follow-up

**The main rule:** a good share of replies come not from your first message but from a follow-up.

### Email sequence (14 days, 4 touches)

```
Day 1: Initial cold message
   ↓ (if no reply)
Day 3: Follow-up #1
   "Wanted to make sure my last note didn't get buried.
   No pressure at all."
   ↓
Day 7: Follow-up #2
   "Sending one more thing that might help, whether or not
   we end up talking: [link to relevant article/case study]"
   ↓
Day 14: Final break-up email
   "Closing the loop. Sounds like it's not the right time.
   The door's open if anything changes. Best of luck with [their project]."
```

**Why 4, not 7:**
- Too many follow-ups come across as pushy and hurt your domain's reputation
- Every touch should **add value**, not repeat "did you see my email?"
- A polite final note makes it easy for people to answer either way, and sometimes that's the one they reply to

### LinkedIn sequence (shorter and more personal)

```
Day 1: Connection request with a short note
Day 3 (if accepted): First message, with something useful
Day 10: Follow-up with a case study or resource
Day 21: Final soft check-in
```

**Don't:**
- ❌ Pitch in the connection request (fewer people accept)
- ❌ Follow up an hour after they accept (it's off-putting)
- ❌ Hit someone on 3+ channels at once (it feels like being followed around)

## A/B testing your messages

Cold outreach without A/B testing is shooting in the dark.

**What to test (in order of priority):**
1. **Subject line** (has the biggest effect on open rate)
2. **First sentence** (affects the reply rate among people who opened)
3. **CTA** ("15-min call?" vs. "worth a chat?" vs. "interested in seeing a demo?")
4. **Length** (50 words vs. 100 words vs. 150 words)

**The process:**
- Test 2 versions at the same time, 50 messages each
- Wait 7 days (including follow-up #1)
- Pick the winner and write 2 new versions
- Repeat for several rounds: your reply rate usually goes up, but how much varies from person to person

**Tool:** platforms like Lemlist have built-in A/B testing, so you don't have to count by hand

🎨 **Picture this:** A/B testing is like dialing in your fishing tackle. You change the lure, cast, and see if anything bites. Nothing? Change it again. A month later you have the right tackle for this particular lake.

## AI for personalization: your 2026 advantage

This can be your biggest edge in 2026. AI speeds up researched outreach a lot without hurting quality, as long as a person checks the result.

**What AI does well:**
- Scans a prospect's LinkedIn profile and pulls out 2-3 hooks
- Reads their recent posts on X or their blog and suggests an opening line
- Analyzes the company website and finds signs of pain
- Drafts a personalized first line for the message

**What AI does BADLY (don't rely on it):**
- A whole cold message from start to finish (readers often recognize template-style AI writing, and you get fewer replies)
- Email subject lines (too generic)
- Sounding like you

**Best practice:** **AI helps with the research, a person writes.** Use Claude to read their LinkedIn profile and give you 3 facts. Then you write the message yourself, using those facts.

**A workflow with Claude Code:**
Read the prospect's and the company's public pages (LinkedIn doesn't allow profiles to be read automatically: open the profile yourself and paste the text into the prompt). Run this from the terminal:

```bash
# Script: research a prospect → generate an opening line
claude -p "Here is the public text of their profile and company website: [paste text].
Extract: recent post topic, company news, role responsibilities.
Suggest 3 opening lines for cold outreach connecting their work to [my solution]."
```

**Tools for automation:**
- **Clay**: AI data enrichment (see the site for pricing)
- **A custom worker**: a small program you write yourself; you pay only for API tokens
- **Lemlist + Liquid syntax**: variables in your templates

## Common pitfalls: 8 mistakes that sink outreach

❌ **Writing to the wrong person**: a junior developer who can't make a purchase
   ✅ Target decision-makers (Head of X, VP, the CEO at small companies)

❌ **Pitching without understanding their business**: one-size-fits-all
   ✅ 2-3 minutes of research before every message

❌ **Asking for a 30-minute call in the first message**: too much
   ✅ "15 minutes" or "quick question": a low barrier

❌ **Long messages (over 200 words)**: TL;DR territory
   ✅ Email under 100 words, LinkedIn under 200, X under 50

❌ **No personalization beyond the first name**: it feels like a mail merge
   ✅ At least one specific fact about their company or their work

❌ **Ignoring recent news**: generic
   ✅ Their recent post, launch, hiring push or funding round makes a perfect hook

❌ **Selling features instead of outcomes**
   ✅ "Gets you your Friday back" instead of "AI-powered automation"

❌ **No clear ask, or a muddy CTA**
   ✅ One specific ask: "15-minute call Thursday?" or a Calendly link

## Legal basics in 2026

Cold outreach isn't banned, but it is regulated. Not knowing the rules doesn't get you off the hook.

**CAN-SPAM (United States), in general terms:**
- Include a physical mailing address in the email footer
- Use an honest subject line (no "Re: our conversation" when there was no conversation)
- Make opting out easy (an unsubscribe link or clear instructions)
- Honor opt-out requests within 10 business days

**GDPR (European Union):**
- The legal basis for B2B cold email depends on the country and the situation (often it's "legitimate interest," but not everywhere)
- Respond quickly to requests to opt out and delete someone's data, within the legal deadlines
- Pseudonymize personal data in your CRM (store codes instead of names where you can)
- Don't use personal (consumer) email addresses (B2C cold email is a gray area)

**Canada (CASL):**
- Commercial messages usually require consent (express, or implied under certain conditions) and a working unsubscribe option
- If you're emailing into Canada or from Canada, check the current text of the law

**Other countries:**
- Many countries have their own personal data laws. A work email address with an employee's name in it can count as personal data too; check with a lawyer

**LinkedIn's terms of service:**
- LinkedIn limits how many invitations and messages you can send; the exact limits aren't published and they change
- Accounts get restricted for mass activity: keep your volume small and do it by hand, with no browser automation
- Sales Navigator gives you more room, but not unlimited room

**The practical rule:**
- A small volume you can handle by hand
- Personalize (it shows you're not sending in bulk)
- Make opting out easy
- Don't try to get around the terms of service

This isn't legal advice: laws change, so before you send anything, check the current rules in your country and in the recipient's country, and ask a lawyer if you're unsure.

## Practice

### Step 1: Find your first 20 prospects (1 hour)

```bash
# Open Apollo (or LinkedIn Sales Navigator, if you have access)
# Filters:
#   - Industry: your ICP industry
#   - Company size: 10-50 employees (for a starter)
#   - Title: your target persona (e.g. "Head of Operations")
#   - Location: your target geography (a country, state or metro area)
# Export 50 contacts to a CSV file
```

Set up a table:
```
| Name | Company | Title | LinkedIn | Email | Status | Last touch |
```

### Step 2: Research your top 5 (1 hour, 12 min each)

For each of your top 5:
- Read their LinkedIn: 3 recent posts
- Check the company blog or news: a recent launch or funding round
- Write down 2-3 personalization hooks in your spreadsheet

### Step 3: Write 5 personalized messages (45 min)

Use a template from this lesson. For each message:
- 2 min on personalization (from your research)
- 5 min on drafting
- 2 min on review (anything pushy, hyped or impossible to back up? Is the ask clear?)

### Step 4: Send (15 min)

- 5 messages on day one
- Follow-up #1 three days later
- Track everything in your spreadsheet

### Step 5: Iterate (a week later)

- How many replies out of 5?
- What worked in the messages that got opened?
- Adjust your template and go again

**Goal for your first month:** send 50 personalized messages and write down what worked. Results depend on your niche and on your work. This is your foundation.

## Key takeaways

> Cold outreach in 2026 isn't 1990s telemarketing. It's a tactful introduction between people who don't know each other yet, where AI helps with the research and a person writes the message. The researched approach usually gets the best reply rate: 5-7 minutes per message.

> The 5-part structure (Hook → Pain → Solution → Proof → Ask) is a formula you can adapt to any channel. Email 50-100 words, LinkedIn 150-200, X (Twitter) 30-50. Drop one part and your reply rate drops.

> You need the volume math: sketch your funnel (messages → replies → calls → clients) using your own conversion rates. A 4-touch sequence matters more than one perfect message, because a real share of replies come from follow-ups.

> AI helps with research (scanning LinkedIn, company news), but **a person writes the message**. People often recognize cold messages written entirely by AI, and fewer of them reply. Two layers: AI cuts your research time, and you keep your human voice.
