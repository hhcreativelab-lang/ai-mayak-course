# Graduation: your 30-60-90 day AI business plan

**Time:** about 20 min reading + 40 min practice

---

## The gist

You know where you're headed: your first paying clients and steady work building AI automations. This plan is your GPS, with every turn marked. At 30 days you reach the first intersection: do you have a first paying client? At 60 you merge onto the highway: do you have your first recurring revenue (money that comes in every month)? At 90 you switch on cruise control: does the system keep running without you pushing it every minute?

⚠️ The numbers in this plan are checkpoints for measuring yourself, not a forecast and not a promise of income. Results depend on your niche, your network, your prices and the time you put in. Some people still have no clients at day 90, and that's useful data for the next step too.

This plan is calmer than the 30-day plan in the lesson [Build-Along: start an AI consulting business](113-build-along-ai-consulting.md): that one is a compressed scenario for a single service, and this is the general order for three months. The steps in both plans are the same. Pick one pace and stick to it.

---

## Key concepts

- **The 30-60-90 plan**: three phases, each with concrete goals and metrics
- **Weekly Review checklist**: a short weekly check on your progress
- **Revenue progression**: how to track your own numbers week by week, without outside forecasts
- **Professional communities and networking**: where the next level comes from once the course is over
- **Your first helper**: when and how to hire one

## Theory

### Why 30-60-90 and not just "get started"

🎨 **Picture this:** a marathon with no mile markers. You run, you get tired, you have no idea how much is left, and you end up slowing down or dropping out. With a marker at every mile, you know where you are, you can adjust your pace, and you can see your progress. The 30-60-90 plan is your set of mile markers.

Without structure, most people get stuck in one of three places:
- **Endless preparation:** "I'll learn a little more, then I'll start talking to clients"
- **The first rejection:** one "no," and it's "I guess this isn't for me"
- **The one-off project:** they built one thing and have no idea how to grow from there

A 30-60-90 plan fixes that:
- It sets concrete goals with deadlines (not "someday")
- It breaks one big goal into three manageable stages
- It gives you fixed points for an honest self-check

🎨 **Picture this:** a GPS route. You know the destination: a steady flow of clients. But a GPS doesn't just say "go over there." It says: "In 500 feet, turn right. In 2 miles, take the exit." Day 30 is the first turn. Day 60 is the on-ramp to the highway. Day 90 is the open road.

---

### Days 1-30: Foundation

🎨 **Picture this:** pouring the foundation for a house. From the street you can't see a thing, and the neighbors walk by thinking you're doing nothing. But without a foundation, the walls come down in the first week. Days 1-30 are the invisible work that holds everything else up.

**Goal of this phase:** be ready for your first paying client

**Weeks 1-2: Portfolio and positioning**

- □ Pick a specialty (1-2 types of service, not everything at once)
  - Example: "Newsletter Automation + Content Pipeline"
  - NOT: "I do any kind of AI project"
- □ Build 1-2 demo projects (from the course exercises or new ones)
  - They need to: actually work, have real metrics, look professional
- □ Write 2 case studies using the Problem → Solution → Result formula (lesson [Portfolio and case studies](41-portfolio-case-studies.md))
  - No real clients yet? Write up your own projects as case studies
- □ Create a portfolio page in Notion (same portfolio lesson)
  - Minimum: About + 2 case studies + Services + Contact

**Weeks 3-4: First conversations**

- □ Fill in your Trust Map (lesson [How to find your first clients](38-monetization-clients.md)): at least 20 contacts
- □ Have 5 Warm Conversations (same lesson on first clients)
  - Goal: learn about their pain points, not sell
- □ Create Basic/Pro/Enterprise packages for your specialty (lesson [Packaging](42-packaging.md))
  - With prices, a scope document and an SLA (what you promise, and how fast)
- □ Do an ROI calculation for at least 2 potential clients (lesson [Monetization case studies](47-monetization-cases.md))
  - Concrete numbers for their pain → value of the solution → price
- □ Have 2-3 sales conversations (not Warm Conversations: this time you propose a project)

**Day 30 milestone:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Case studies in your portfolio | 2+ |
| Warm Conversations | 5+ |
| Sales conversations | 2-3 |
| First paying client | Nice to have (days 31-60 are still ahead) |

**If you don't have a client by day 30, that's normal.** The time it takes to land a first client is different for everyone. What matters is that your pipeline (the flow of potential clients, from first contact to a signed deal) is up and running.

---

### Days 31-60: Traction

🎨 **Picture this:** the first push on a bike's pedals. Standing still, the bike goes nowhere. The first two or three turns of the pedals are hard and wobbly. By the fifth, you're balancing on your own. Your first client is those first pedal strokes. After that, you're riding.

**Goal of this phase:** your first delivered project plus your first recurring revenue

**Your first paying client:**

If you already have one by day 30, great. If not, keep the pipeline from phase 1 going and add:

- □ Cold outreach (lesson [Cold outreach with Claude](45-cold-outreach.md)): 10-15 personalized emails a week
  - Focus: the same niche as your warm contacts
- □ LinkedIn activity: 3-4 posts a week about your work
  - Show the process: "Today I launched a system that..." (screenshots!)
  - Practice, not theory
- □ First project: even at a reduced price (the first experience matters more than the price)

**Delivering your first project:**

- □ Use the handoff protocol from the lesson [How to hand off a project and keep the client](46-delivery-retention.md)
- □ Record a Loom walkthrough video
- □ Ask for a testimonial and an NPS score (a 0-10 "would you recommend me?" rating) right after the demo
- □ Ask for referrals ("Is there anyone else who could use something like this?")

**Your first recurring revenue:**

- □ After delivery: offer a maintenance contract at a fixed monthly price
  - "Includes monitoring, API updates (when the connected services change), and 30 minutes of consulting a month"
- □ Send the client a monthly report with numbers (template in the same delivery lesson)
  - It keeps the client and reminds them why they're paying

**Day 60 milestone:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Projects delivered | 1-2 |
| First paying client | Yes |
| Maintenance contract | 1 |
| Case studies with real clients | 1+ |
| Trust Map conversations | 15+ |

---

### Days 61-90: Scale

🎨 **Picture this:** a train picking up speed. For the first hundred yards, the locomotive pulls with everything it has, and it still moves slowly. Once it's rolling, keeping that speed takes a fraction of the energy it took to build it. In days 61-90 you're already moving; the job is not to stop.

**Goal of this phase:** 3+ clients, your Factory Model up and running

**Factory Model:**

- □ Create a template for your main type of project (lesson [Factory Model](48-factory-model.md))
  - Goal: the next project of this type goes 2x faster
- □ An SOP (standard operating procedure: a written step-by-step) for each type: 5 steps, 5 days, exactly what you do
- □ Update your prices: with the Factory → a higher price for the same or less time

**Growing your pipeline:**

- □ Cold outreach: 15-20 emails a week (personalized with Claude)
- □ Ask your first clients for referrals
- □ Consider one networking event (online or in person)
  - Not to sell, but to understand the market and get seen

**Optimization:**

- □ A weekly review every Friday (15 minutes):
  - How many conversations this week?
  - How many proposals sent?
  - How many projects in the pipeline?
  - What went wrong, and what will I change?
- □ Revisit your positioning based on real feedback from clients
  - What do they value most? What do they ask about most often?

**Day 90 milestone:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Projects delivered | 3-5 |
| Active clients | 3+ |
| Recurring monthly revenue | Growing month over month |
| Factory templates | 1-2 |
| Average delivery time | < 7 days (vs. 14 at the start) |

---

### Revenue progression: how to read your own numbers

🎨 **Picture this:** a snowball rolling down a hill. For the first few feet you push it by hand, and it stays small. The farther it goes, the bigger it gets and the faster it rolls on its own. Referrals from your first clients, a Factory that speeds up delivery, a reputation in your niche: all of it keeps building on itself once the system is running.

The plan's sequence looks like this, with no dollar forecasts:

```
Day 30:      first conversations, 1-2 demo case studies, maybe a first project
Day 60:      1-2 delivered projects, a first maintenance contract
Day 90:      3+ clients, a Factory template, referrals start coming in
Month 6+:    a reputation in your niche, repeat orders
```

**An important caveat:** this is not a forecast and not a promise of income. Timelines and amounts are different for everyone. The one thing they have in common: results come from actively working on the business, not from a wait-and-see mode. Track your own numbers in the Weekly Review below, and compare them only with your own numbers from last week.

**What affects your speed:**
- A warm network of contacts (do you have one?) → faster
- Prior experience in sales or working with clients → faster
- A niche where you understand how the business works → faster
- Starting from zero, with no network → slower, but doable

### Weekly Review checklist

Every Friday, 15 minutes:

```markdown
## Week [N]: [Date]

### This week's numbers
- Warm/sales conversations held: ___
- Cold emails sent: ___
- Replies received: ___ (___%)
- Projects in progress: ___
- Projects delivered: ___
- Income this week: $___

### What worked
[One or two things that got results]

### What didn't work
[One or two things that didn't pay off]

### The one change for next week
[One concrete action]

### Drift check
□ Am I still focused on my niche, or am I branching out into everything?
□ Am I having conversations with clients, or just polishing the product?
□ Do I ask for referrals after every successful project?
```

This one checklist, once a week, is enough to keep you on course.

### Communities and networking beyond the course: how to keep going

After 90 days you have a base. The next level is visibility and connections:

**Online communities:**
- Facebook groups, Slack communities and newsletters for business owners in your niche (not AI groups: the business niche itself)
- LinkedIn: post a case study or a lesson you learned once a week
- Reddit (r/artificial for general context, niche subreddits to find clients)

**In person:**
- Meetup groups for business owners in your city, and your local chamber of commerce
- Industry-specific conferences (not AI conferences: go where your clients are)

**The community rule:** give value before you ask for anything. Answer questions, share what you've learned. After 2-3 months of steady activity, people may start recommending you on their own.

### When to hire your first helper

🎨 **Picture this:** a chef opens a restaurant. At first she cooks, takes orders and washes the dishes herself. At some point there's a line out the door, and she's stuck at the sink. That's when she hires someone for the dishes so she can cook. Hire before there's a line, and you're paying a dishwasher while the dining room sits empty.

Signs it's time:

- □ Client income has steadily covered your expenses plus a helper's pay for 2+ months in a row
- □ You're turning down projects because you don't have the time
- □ More than 50% of your time goes to operations, not sales or strategy
- □ You have a Factory with documented SOPs

What to hand off first:
- Initial research and data gathering to personalize outreach
- Basic testing of workflows after you build them
- Writing documentation and SOPs
- Monthly client reports (from a template)

Who to look for:
- A freelancer with a basic understanding of AI and APIs
- Upwork / Contra / Toptal for technical tasks
- A virtual assistant for admin work

If you're not sure how to pay a freelancer the right way (contracts, tax paperwork), ask your accountant before you hire: the rules differ by state and by country.

**Don't hire before your Factory is working.** Delegating chaos means paying for chaos.

## Practice

**Exercise: write your own 30-60-90 plan**

1. Fill in the plan for yourself:

**Days 1-30:**
```
My specialty: ____________
Portfolio goal: ___ case studies
Conversations goal: ___ warm + ___ sales
First client goal: $___
```

**Days 31-60:**
```
Outreach plan: ___ emails/week
LinkedIn plan: ___ posts/week (what will I show?)
First maintenance contract goal: $___/month
```

**Days 61-90:**
```
Factory plan: which template first?
3rd client plan: where will they come from? (referrals / outreach / LinkedIn?)
Revenue goal by day 90: $___/month
```

2. Fill in the Weekly Review for the current week (even if you're just starting, it's your baseline)

3. Set a recurring calendar reminder: every Friday at 5:00 p.m., "Weekly Review (15 min)"

4. Put three checkpoint dates on your calendar: +30 days, +60 days, +90 days. On each one, check your metrics.

**Goal:** a concrete, personal plan with dates and numbers. Not "I'll do my best," but "by June 8, I'll have 2 case studies and 5 conversations."

## Common mistakes

- **A plan without real dates.** "I'll look for clients" isn't a plan. "By June 8: 5 warm conversations, 2 sales conversations, 1 case study in the portfolio" is a plan. Put real dates on your calendar.
- **Working without numbers.** Without the Weekly Review (15 minutes!), you don't know whether the plan is working. How many conversations? How many replies to outreach? How much did you earn? If you don't measure it, you can't manage it.
- **Improving the product instead of selling.** The trap: "I'll polish the portfolio / template / demo a little more." Sales means talking to people. 1 hour of conversations > 5 hours of polishing.
- **Quitting after the first rejection.** Only a small share of people reply to cold emails, and an even smaller share become clients. That's normal math: track your own conversion rate in the Weekly Review, not someone else's.
- **Hiring a helper before the Factory.** Delegating chaos = paying for chaos. First SOPs + templates, then delegation.

## Key takeaways

> A 30-60-90 plan is a GPS, not a railroad track. Direction matters more than following it to the letter. No client by day 30 isn't a failure; it's data.

> Your first client matters more than your first big check. A small first project that gives you real experience and a testimonial is worth more than a theoretical plan for a big sum.

> The Weekly Review is the most valuable 15 minutes of your week. Without it, it's easy to spend a month feeling busy while making no real progress.

> Hire a helper after the Factory, not before. Delegating chaos means paying for chaos.
