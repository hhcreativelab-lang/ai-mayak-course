# Lead magnets: a simple funnel from free to paid

**Time:** about 30 min reading + 45 min practice

The percentages and dollar amounts in this lesson are rough reference points for working through the method. They're not market statistics and not a promise of results. Measure your own numbers. Tool prices were checked as of October 2026 where that's noted; for everything else, check the tools' websites.

---

## The gist

You have a product. You sold it to your first 5 to 10 clients through cold outreach (see the lesson [Cold outreach in 2026](39c-cold-outreach-deep.md)). That approach doesn't scale: cold messages take your time every single day. You need a system that keeps bringing in interested people even when you're not actively selling.

A lead magnet is the free sample in a store. You walk into a tea shop, and they pour you a little cup to taste. Like it? You buy a tin. Don't like it? You walk out. Nobody pressures you, calls you later or "walks you through a presentation." The shop makes its money on the share of people who liked what they tasted.

Online it works the same way: someone downloads a free PDF, uses a calculator, or takes a 7-day mini-course by email. They get something useful right away. They start to trust you. After 5 to 10 emails, some of them become clients. Not everyone. The goal is enough of them for the funnel to pay for itself.

This lesson covers how to build a funnel like that from scratch in 30 days: which lead magnet to pick, which emails to write, what it costs and which numbers to watch. And most important, how not to kill trust with a hard sell in the very first email.

🎨 **Picture this:** a tea shop with free tastings vs. the kiosk salesperson at the mall who steps into your path. The kiosk salesperson (cold outreach) grabs people as they walk by: it's exhausting, few people buy, and your reputation takes a hit. The shop with tastings works around the clock: people walk in on their own, those who like it buy, and everyone else leaves with no hard feelings. You build it once, and it can keep working for years.

## 🎯 When you need a lead magnet (and when you don't)

A lead magnet works if:
- ✓ You have a product (not an idea, but a real product people pay for)
- ✓ You have 5+ paying clients (you understand who buys and why)
- ✓ You want to grow through inbound (people coming to you), not through cold outreach
- ✓ You're ready to put in 30 days of setup and then 1 to 2 hours a week of tweaking

You DON'T need a lead magnet if:
- ✗ You don't have a product yet. First, validate it with cold outreach (the lesson [Cold outreach in 2026](39c-cold-outreach-deep.md))
- ✗ You have fewer than 5 clients. You don't know your audience well enough yet to build a funnel
- ✗ You sell very expensive enterprise contracts. There, relationships and referrals do the work, not funnels
- ✗ You want "fast clients this week." A funnel takes at least 2 to 3 months to warm up

**Default:** if you got your first 10 clients through outreach and want a predictable flow, it's time to build a funnel.

## Key concepts

- **Lead magnet**: a free resource people get in exchange for their email address. It should give them something concrete and useful in 5 to 15 minutes.
- **Landing page**: one page, one call to action: "enter your email, get the resource." No menus, no links, no distractions.
- **Opt-in conversion**: the percentage of landing page visitors who leave their email (who "opt in"). A rough benchmark: 20-30%; yours may differ.
- **Email sequence**: a series of emails (usually 5 to 8) sent automatically after someone opts in. The goal: give value → build trust → offer the paid product.
- **Nurture**: "warming up" a cold lead (a potential client who doesn't know you yet) with a series of useful emails before you sell anything.
- **Funnel conversion**: the percentage of people who go all the way from first contact to a purchase. For cold traffic it's usually a fraction of a percent or a few percent; calculate your own.
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**: how much it costs to bring in one paying client through the funnel.

## Theory

### 5 types of lead magnets in 2026 (by conversion rate)

Not all lead magnets are equal. Below are relative reference points (which ones rank higher, which lower), not measured market statistics:

| Type | Conversion (email signup) | Time to make | Quality of lead |
|------|--------------------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Quick Tool** (an AI calculator, a generator) | high | 2-3 days | HIGH (active interest) |
| **Cheat Sheet PDF** | medium | 1 day | MEDIUM |
| **Email Mini-course** (7 days) | medium | 4-5 days | HIGH (warm by day 7) |
| **Webinar / Video** | below medium | 5-7 days | HIGH if attended |
| **Template Pack** | medium | 2-3 days | MEDIUM-HIGH |

**What to pick as a beginner:** a cheat sheet PDF is the fastest start. One day of work, decent conversion. A tool brings in better-quality leads, but you have to build it.

**Anti-pattern:** "Free 30-minute consultation." It sounds valuable, but a cold audience isn't ready to give up that much time. Conversion is usually the lowest of all.

🎨 **Picture this:** a tasting at the tea shop takes 3 seconds: you try it, and you either like it or you don't. A "free consultation" is like saying "come drink tea with us for 30 minutes." Who's going to sit down with a stranger for that? The lower the barrier, the more people walk in.

### Ready-made lead magnet ideas for AI niches

Concrete ideas for AI consultants, content creators and coaches.

**For AI consultants (B2B):**
1. **"AI Readiness Assessment"**: a 20-question quiz → a personalized PDF report. High conversion, high quality (the person spent several minutes thinking, so they're interested).
2. **"AI ROI Calculator"**: someone enters their current process (hours, hourly rate, volume) → gets an estimate of what automation could save them. The tool works around the clock without you.
3. **"5 AI Workflows for [niche] PDF"**: for one specific niche (accountants, lawyers, marketers). Specificity = conversion.

**For content creators / SaaS (B2C):**
4. **"100 ChatGPT prompts for [niche]"**: a mass-market magnet, easy to make. Conversion can be good, but the leads are colder.
5. **"AI Content Calendar Template"** (Notion / Google Sheets): a practical tool people keep using for weeks.

**For coaches / online courses:**
6. **"Mini-course: 7 days to your first AI workflow"**: a series of email lessons. A warm lead by the end of the week.

**Rule:** a lead magnet should be the **logical first step** toward your paid product. If you sell a course on AI automation, a calculator showing "how much you're losing without automation" fits perfectly. If you sell design work, "100 prompts for AI image generation" is off target.

### Anatomy of a high-converting lead magnet

A high-converting magnet has 5 traits. If even one is missing, conversion drops noticeably.

1. **Specific**: not "Learn AI" but "5 AI workflows for accountants that save 10 hours/week." The title answers three questions: who it's for, what they get and what result to expect.

2. **Quick win**: value in 5 to 15 minutes. Not a 50-page ebook (nobody will read it). A 3- to 5-page PDF, a tool that works in 30 seconds, a 7-minute video.

3. **Aligned to the product**: the lead magnet should lead logically to your paid product. The paid product solves a bigger version of the same problem.

4. **Visual / branded**: design quality affects whether someone decides "it's worth giving my email." A Canva template beats a plain Word doc. One font, 2 colors, a clean layout.

5. **Auto-delivered**: instantly, by email, right after they sign up. No "we'll get back to you within 24 hours." People want the result now.

🎨 **Picture this:** the tasting at the tea shop has to be ready and nicely set out, so a person can try it in 3 seconds. If it's "hang on, we'll brew some now," they'll leave.

### Full funnel architecture

The complete map of the path from stranger to client:

```
Cold Traffic
(X (Twitter) / LinkedIn / Reddit / paid ads / SEO)
            ↓
   Landing Page
(one headline, one promise, one field: email)
            ↓
   Email opt-in
            ↓
   Welcome Email
(instant: delivers the magnet + a short intro of who you are)
            ↓
   Email 2-7
(one a day: tip → case → myth → DIY → soft pitch)
            ↓
   Email 8
(strong offer: paid product / consultation)
            ↓
       Customer
            ↓
   (next: onboarding sequence, upsell, referral)
```

**Where people drop off (a made-up example; you'll measure your own numbers later):**
- most landing page visitors leave without giving an email (normal)
- some people who gave an email won't open the first one (normal: spam folder, busy)
- some who opened won't make it to email 7 (normal: unsubscribed, lost interest)
- a small share of those who remain will buy (normal for cold traffic)

**Example calculation:** 1,000 cold visitors → 200 emails → 100 active readers → a few customers. Plug in your own conversion rates: that shows you exactly where the funnel leaks.

### 7-day email sequence template (copy-paste)

A ready-made skeleton for the emails. Adapt it to your niche; don't copy it blindly.

**Email 1 (instant after opt-in): Deliver + Welcome**

```
Subject: Your [magnet name] is inside 👇

Hi!

Thanks for signing up. Here's your [magnet]. Open it right now:
[LINK]

In a 5-minute read you'll learn [specific benefit 1, 2, 3].

I'm [your name], [short positioning]. I help [audience] with [problem].

Tomorrow at this time I'll send you the first practical tip:
[teaser for the topic of email 2].

Talk soon,
[Name]

P.S. If my emails aren't reaching you, check your spam folder. Add me
to your contacts so you don't miss the next ones.
```

**Email 2 (day 1, 9:00 a.m.): Tip 1 + personal story**

```
Subject: The most common mistake [audience] make with [topic]

When I started working with [audience] clients, many of them made
the same mistake: [specific mistake].

I made it too. It cost me [a specific number: time / money / results]
until I realized I needed to [right approach].

Here's how to do it right:
1. [step 1]
2. [step 2]
3. [step 3]

Try it today. It takes 15 minutes.

Tomorrow I'll tell you how one of my clients used this approach and
[specific result].

[Name]
```

**Email 3 (day 2): Case study**

```
Subject: How [client name / archetype] saved [X hours / dollars]

A real story (name changed).

Maria, a bookkeeper in [city]. She did [process] by hand every month.
6 hours of work. And by the end of every month, she was worn out.

We set up [solution]. What changed:
- Before: 6 hours, mistakes, stress
- After: 30 minutes, a quick check, peace of mind

The key wasn't the tool. It was how we set it up.
More on that tomorrow.

[Name]
```

**Email 4 (day 3): Myth bust**

```
Subject: "AI will replace [profession]." It won't.

The most common myth I hear from [audience]: "AI is going to replace everyone soon."

The reality in 2026: AI isn't replacing people, it's replacing the **boring parts of the job**.
Good [profession]s who use it get more done. Those who skip it
keep doing the tedious parts by hand.

The difference:
- A good [profession] + AI = more done in less time
- A [profession] who ignores it = still doing the boring parts by hand

Tomorrow I'll show you where to start.

[Name]
```

**Email 5 (day 4): DIY (build trust by giving)**

```
Subject: Do it yourself: [specific task] in 1 hour

Not everyone needs a consultant. If you have a free hour,
here's how to do [task] on your own.

Step 1: [action]
Step 2: [action]
Step 3: [action]
Step 4: [check]

If it works, great: you just saved [amount].
If you get stuck, tomorrow I'll tell you what to do next.

[Name]
```

**Email 6 (day 5): Soft pitch**

```
Subject: If you'd like my help

Over the last 5 emails I've shown you [what you gave them].

Most people take this and apply it on their own. That's great;
that's exactly what this mini-course was made for.

But if you [trigger: "don't want to spend 40 hours teaching yourself" /
"need results by [date]" / "already tried and got stuck"], I can help.

I do [short description of the service]. It's $[price] / [period].
What's included: [3 points].

If you're interested, reply to this email and we'll see whether it's a fit.
No pressure. Not everyone needs it.

[Name]
```

**Email 7 (day 6): Strong CTA**

```
Subject: I'm closing enrollment for [month] on Friday

Short version: I work with [N] clients at a time. This month
[N-X] spots are taken.

If you've been thinking about starting, this is the last day I can take
on a new client until [date + 4 weeks].

Sign up: [link / "reply to this email"]

If now isn't the right time, no problem. The next opening is
[date]. I'll let you know.

[Name]

P.S. Thanks for spending these 7 days with me. Even if you don't buy,
I hope you put at least one tip to use.
```

Only mention limited spots if the limit is real. Made-up scarcity and urgency kill trust.

⚠️ Email only people who signed up themselves (never a bought or scraped list), and keep the unsubscribe link your email platform adds to every message. Marketing email has rules about consent and unsubscribing; your email platform's help center explains the basics.

**Principle:** 5 emails give value, 2 sell. Not the other way around.

### Funnel metrics to track

What to measure, and when to worry.

| Stage | Rough benchmark | Action if low |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| Landing page → email signup | 20-30% | Improve the headline / simplify the form |
| Email opens (avg) | 30-45% | Better subject lines / sender name |
| Email clicks | 5-10% | Less "salesy," a better call to action |
| Email → paid customer | 1-3% | Better offer / pricing / nurture |
| Overall funnel (cold) | 0.3-1% | Acceptable. Otherwise, review the whole flow |

These are starting points for a first comparison, not a guarantee: every niche and channel has its own numbers. Over time, your own numbers become the benchmark that matters most.

**Where to look:**
- Kit and Beehiiv show open and click rates automatically
- Landing page conversion: Plausible or PostHog
- Sales: your CRM (software for tracking clients and deals) or just a Google Sheet with UTM tags (short labels added to a link that show where a visitor came from)

**Review cadence:**
- First 30 days: every week
- After that: every 2 weeks
- Don't optimize on small numbers (<100 signups): it's noise

### Cost economics

What it costs to launch and keep running.

**Setup (one-time):**
- Carrd from $19/year: the landing page
- Canva (free plan available): designing the lead magnet
- Creating the lead magnet: your time, 2 to 5 days
- **Total:** about $20-30 in cash to start + 30 hours of your time

**Monthly:**
- Kit free plan; a paid plan once you grow or need its features
- Plausible from $9/month (optional at the start)
- Hosting the magnet: from zero to a few dollars
- **Total:** from zero to a few dozen dollars a month in the first months, more as you grow

**Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):**
- **Organic traffic** (X (Twitter) / LinkedIn / Reddit / SEO): no money needed, but it takes hours a week of content
- **Paid ads** (Meta / Google / LinkedIn): the cost of an email subscriber and of a client depends on your niche and channel. Run a small test and calculate yours

**Best ratio:**
- Organic content → email collection → nurture → sale
- A client's LTV (lifetime value: how much a client pays you over the whole time they stay with you) should be **at least 3x CAC**. If LTV is $300 and CAC is $100, you're fine. If LTV is $300 and CAC is $250, you have a problem.

🎨 **Picture this:** back to the tea shop. Say its space costs $500 a month, tastings are free, and the average sale is $30. To cover the rent, it needs at least 17 buyers a month. A funnel works the same way. Run the unit economics before you start sending traffic.

### Inbound traffic sources 2026

Where to get people to your landing page. Ordered from most to least effective for AI niches.

**X (formerly Twitter, organic):**
- One of the channels where the AI audience is active
- Pattern: value-driven threads, built up over 6+ months
- You need an audience first: there won't be quick results
- No money needed, but it takes hours a week of content

**LinkedIn:**
- Best for B2B (consulting, enterprise SaaS)
- Pattern: a weekly post + personal DMs
- Slower, but higher-quality leads

**YouTube:**
- Evergreen long-form videos that pay off over months
- The audience builds slowly
- The highest barrier to entry, but the most stable long-term channel

**SEO (Google):**
- In 2026 it works more slowly because of AI search (ChatGPT, Perplexity)
- But for long-tail searches and B2B it still works
- Results don't come right away; it usually takes months

**Reddit:**
- Niche subreddits (r/ChatGPT, r/MachineLearning, r/SaaS)
- Only genuinely helpful posts and comments, no open promotion (people get banned fast for promoting)
- High-quality leads if you hit your niche

**Paid (Meta / Google / LinkedIn Ads):**
- The fastest, but the most expensive
- Figure out the cost of a paying client from a test
- Turn it on only after your organic funnel has proven that it works and that a client pays back what it costs to get them

**Recommendation for beginners:** one organic channel (pick the one where your audience already spends time; for some niches that's Instagram, TikTok or Facebook groups) + an email funnel. Paid ads come later, once your unit economics are proven.

### Anti-patterns

The top mistakes that kill a funnel:

- ❌ **A generic 50-page ebook**: nobody reads it, no value delivered, no trust built
- ❌ **A lead magnet not connected to your paid product**: very low conversion to a purchase
- ❌ **A "free trial" instead of a real lead magnet**: too big a commitment for a cold audience
- ❌ **A hard pitch in emails 1-2**: kills trust, and the unsubscribe rate goes up
- ❌ **No follow-up sequence**: a single welcome email sells almost nothing
- ❌ **Daily emails for 4+ weeks**: a waterfall of unsubscribes, and your emails start landing in spam
- ❌ **No segmentation**: people who downloaded the AI calculator and people who finished the mini-course get the same emails
- ❌ **A weak landing page**: long text, 5 calls to action, a distracting menu. It should be: hook → benefit → form. That's it.
- ❌ **Not tracking the numbers**: you're flying blind, with nothing to optimize

### A/B testing your funnel

An A/B test means showing two versions to different visitors and comparing the results. What to test (in order of impact):

1. **Lead magnet title**: the biggest lever. "5 AI workflows" vs. "AI Automation Guide" can noticeably change your opt-in rate.
2. **Landing page headline**: test benefit vs. curiosity vs. specificity.
3. **Send times**: Tuesday 9 a.m. vs. Thursday 2 p.m. vs. Sunday 6 p.m. It depends on your audience.
4. **Subject lines**: curiosity ("I almost deleted this email...") vs. benefit ("3 prompts that save an hour a day").
5. **CTA copy** (the text on the button or link): "Get the guide" vs. "Send it to me" vs. "Yes, I want this."

**A/B rules:**
- Test **one thing at a time**
- At least 100 visitors per version before the result means anything
- At least 3 rounds of changes before you decide the funnel "works"
- Don't test on small numbers: the noise is louder than the signal

## Practice

### Step 1: Pick a lead magnet in 1 day

Don't spend a week thinking about it. Take one of the ready-made ideas above or adapt one.

```
Fill this in (in your notes app):

My niche: ____________________
My product: ____________________
The pain I solve: ____________________

Lead magnet (specific + quick win):
Title: ____________________
Format: PDF / Tool / Email course / Template
Time to deliver value: 5-15 minutes
Logical bridge to the product: ____________________

If you're stuck, ask yourself: what would I want to get for free
from an expert in my niche? Make that.
```

---

### Step 2: Create the lead magnet in 2-3 days

For a PDF:
```
1. Open Canva and pick a "Lead Magnet" / "Workbook" template
2. Structure (5 pages is enough):
   - Cover (title + your name)
   - What's inside (table of contents)
   - Content, part 1
   - Content, part 2
   - CTA (what to do next + how to reach you)
3. Brand: one font, 2 colors, a consistent style
4. Export → PDF
5. Upload to Cloudflare R2 / S3 / Google Drive (public link)
```

For a tool:
```
1. A simple calculator in HTML + JS
2. Deploy to Cloudflare Pages (there's a free plan)
3. Capture the email before showing the result
4. Webhook → Kit (subscriber added)
```

---

### Step 3: Set up the landing page in 1 day

```
1. Carrd → new site → a "Lead capture" template
2. Headline: [benefit-driven, specific to your audience]
   Example: "Download 5 AI workflows for accountants and
   save 10 hours a week"
3. Subheadline: one line on who it's for and why
4. Form:
   - Email (only!)
   - Button: "Send it to me" / "Get the PDF"
5. Connect Kit (formerly ConvertKit) through the integration
6. Set up the confirmation email (with a Kit automation)
7. Custom domain (optional; the price depends on the registrar)
```

---

### Step 4: Write the email sequence in 3-4 days

```
1. Use the template above (Emails 1-7)
2. Adapt it to your voice and niche
3. In Kit:
   - Create a Sequence "[Magnet name] Nurture"
   - Add 7 emails
   - Set the delays: Email 1 instant, Emails 2-7 one per day
4. Create an Automation:
   - Trigger: subscribed to form "[Magnet name]"
   - Action: add to sequence
5. Test: sign up with your own test email and check all 7 emails
```

---

### Step 5: Drive your first 100 visitors

```
Choose ONE channel for your first round:

Twitter/X:
- 5 threads in 2 weeks in your niche
- Each thread ends with a soft call to action pointing to the lead magnet
- "Free guide linked in my bio"

LinkedIn:
- 1 post a day for 14 days in a row
- Value right in the post (not "click here for the guide")
- Link to the magnet in your profile and in the comments

Reddit:
- Find 3 relevant subreddits
- Leave helpful comments on 20 posts
- Mention the magnet only when it's relevant (no spam)

Track UTMs:
- [your-site]/?utm_source=twitter
- [your-site]/?utm_source=linkedin
```

---

### Step 6: Measure and iterate over 30 days

```
Week 1: let the data build up (at least 100 visitors)
Week 2: first numbers
  - Opt-in conversion (target 20%+)
  - Email open rate (target 35%+)
  - Click rate (target 7%+)
  - Replies / questions (a good sign!)

Week 3: first round of changes
  - What's weakest? Change ONE thing
  - If opt-in is <15%: change the headline / lead magnet title
  - If opens are <25%: change the subject lines
  - If clicks are <5%: change the CTA copy

Week 4: second round + first sales
  - If you have 1-2 purchases, the stack works: scale up traffic
  - If 0 purchases with 50+ subscribers: review emails 6-7
  - If 0 subscribers: review the landing page + traffic quality
```

## Key takeaways

> A lead magnet isn't "a free course in exchange for an email." It's the first step toward a paid solution to the same problem. If the magnet and the product aren't logically connected, conversion to sales will be very low. The connection should be obvious from the title alone.

> 5 emails give value, 2 sell. Not the other way around. Trust is built by giving; the sale happens after the person has already gotten something useful. If you pitch in emails 1-2, you lose most of that trust.

> A funnel doesn't work on small numbers. Don't optimize on 30 visitors: that's noise. Get at least 100 visitors through the stack before your first round of changes, and at least 1,000 visitors before serious A/B testing. Patience here pays off in data.

> Cold traffic → paying customer: conversion is usually small, so work from your own numbers: how many people came, how many bought, what the traffic cost and what a client's LTV is. Run the unit economics BEFORE you scale (see the lesson [Unit economics made simple](d01-unit-economics-simple.md)).
