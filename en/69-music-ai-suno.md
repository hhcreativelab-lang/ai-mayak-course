# AI music with Suno and sound design

**Time:** about 20 min reading + 30 min practice

---

## The gist

For a business owner, AI music isn't about becoming a musician. It's about covering three specific needs: background music for videos, jingles for your brand, and the sound package for a podcast. And all of that without hiring a separate composer or sound designer.

🎨 **Picture this:** a brand jingle used to mean hiring a musician and a studio, several meetings, a couple of weeks and a separate budget. Now it's 10 minutes in Suno: a prompt, a couple of versions to choose from, and the track is ready. It's not better or worse than live music. It's a different tool for a different scale.

---

## Key concepts

- Suno AI: full tracks from a text prompt (a prompt is your request to the AI), vocals included
- Udio: another AI music service; since October 2025, downloading songs there has been turned off (see the Udio section)
- A music prompt: genre + mood + instruments + tempo + length
- Claude helps you write lyrics (the words of the song) and prompts for Suno
- Commercial use: the rules vary in 2026, so it's important to check them
- ElevenLabs Sound Effects: short sounds for apps, interfaces and podcasts

## Theory

### Why a business owner needs AI music

**Three main use cases:**

**1. Background music for YouTube and other videos**
Stock music costs money or comes with license terms, and lots of other people use the same tracks. AI music is one of a kind, generated to fit the mood of a specific video, and on a paid plan with commercial rights you can use it in monetized content.

**2. A brand jingle**
A short 10-30 second track with your brand's sound. It used to be expensive and slow. Now it's a few prompts: you pick the best one and adjust it.

**3. Sound for a podcast or a course**
An intro and outro, transition music between sections, background atmosphere for teaching segments.

🎨 **Picture this:** music in a video is like the lighting in a restaurant. You don't consciously notice bad lighting, but you feel uncomfortable. The right lighting creates atmosphere and coziness and makes you want to stay. Background music works the same way.

### Suno AI: full tracks from a prompt

Suno creates complete tracks, including:

- The instrumental part
- Vocals (in many languages)
- Song structure: verse, chorus, bridge

**How it works:**
Go to [suno.com](https://suno.com) → "Create" → write a prompt → get two versions → listen → keep going or download.

**Plans (as of October 2026):**

- Free: no cost, 50 credits a day, the v6-mini model, no commercial rights. You can listen to your tracks and share a link, but you only get a few trial downloads in total
- Pro: from $8/month when you pay for a year, 2,500 credits a month, commercial rights, a monthly limit on song downloads
- Premier: from $24/month when you pay for a year, 10,000 credits a month, a higher download limit, Suno Studio
- The price depends on how you pay (yearly or monthly) and may be different in your country. Exact prices and terms: [suno.com/pricing](https://suno.com/pricing), [What's current](https://aimayak.com/en/now/)

**Modes** (the names in the interface change):

- **Custom**: you write the lyrics yourself and pick a style
- **Simple**: you describe the mood, and Suno writes the words itself

### Prompts for Suno: the formula

A music prompt follows a formula:

```
[genre] [tempo] [mood] [instruments] [special details]
```

**Examples of good prompts:**

```
# Background music for a business video
upbeat corporate background music, 120bpm, 
optimistic and professional, piano and strings, 
no vocals, suitable for YouTube

# Brand jingle
catchy brand jingle, 15 seconds, 
energetic and memorable, 
electronic with guitar, 
male vocal humming, modern startup vibe

# Podcast intro
podcast intro music, 30 seconds, 
lo-fi hip hop, chill and focused, 
soft beat with vinyl crackle,
fade in fade out, no vocals

# Transition music for a course
educational ambient music, 10 seconds,
calm and focused, soft piano,
neutral mood, suitable for online course transitions
```

**What works:**

- A specific tempo (BPM, beats per minute)
- The length you want (Suno doesn't always stick to it; trim the extra in an editor)
- Saying "no vocals" if you don't need singing
- The purpose (for YouTube / for podcast / for e-learning)

**What doesn't work:**

- "Make some nice music": too vague
- Listing 10+ parameters: the model gets confused
- Styles that don't go together ("jazz metal ambient")

### Udio: what changed

Udio ([udio.com](https://udio.com)) used to be Suno's competitor. On October 29, 2025, the service announced a partnership with Universal Music Group, and since then downloading audio, video and stems (the separate vocal and instrument tracks) has been turned off: the music stays inside the platform. A licensed platform was announced for 2026. Check the website to see where it stands today and whether downloads have come back.

🎨 **Picture this:** as of October 2026, Udio is like a recording studio where you can listen all you want, but they won't let you take the tape home. For tasks where you need a file (background music for a video, a jingle, sound for a course), Udio doesn't work today.

**Alternatives to Suno as of October 2026:** Google Lyria in the Gemini app (tracks up to three minutes, with or without vocals; Google's pages don't state the terms for commercial use, so read the service's policy) and Soundraw (background music; according to the service, the tracks you create come with a license for commercial use). Compare them on your own prompts, and read the license before you monetize anything.

### Claude helps you write lyrics and prompts

Claude doesn't generate music itself, but it's an excellent helper for the preparation:

**1. Lyrics for Custom Mode in Suno:**

```
Write lyrics for a short brand jingle.
Brand: [name]
What it does: [what the business does]
Length: 30 seconds (4-6 lines)
Style: upbeat, catchy, no clichés
Language: English

Format: verse (4 lines) + chorus (2 lines)
```

**2. Polishing a prompt for Suno:**

```
I need background music for a YouTube channel about [topic].
Audience: [description]
Mood of the videos: [description]

Write 3 versions of a Suno prompt in this format:
[genre] [tempo] [mood] [instruments] [special details]
Each version should be a different style, all for the same purpose.
```

**3. Organizing a sound library:**

```
I have 10 tracks for my YouTube channel.
Help me set up a naming and tagging system:
- by mood (energetic / calm / focused / inspiring)
- by length (intro / transition / background / outro)
- by video type (tutorial / vlog / announcement)
```

### Commercial use: the rules as of October 2026

**Suno:**

- Free: no commercial use; only a few trial downloads, for personal use
- Pro and Premier: commercial rights to the songs you download as a paying subscriber, and a limited number of downloads per month
- Important: Suno can change its terms, so always read the current Terms of Service on the website

**Udio:**

- Song downloads are turned off; check the monetization terms on the website before using anything

**The general rule:**
If a video is monetized on YouTube or the content is used to sell something, you need a paid plan with commercial rights (on Suno, that's Pro or Premier). Save a screenshot of the terms on the day you create the track. This isn't legal advice: if you're unsure, ask a lawyer.

### ElevenLabs Sound Effects: short sounds

ElevenLabs ([elevenlabs.io/sound-effects](https://elevenlabs.io/sound-effects)) generates short sound effects from a text description (see the service's page for length limits).

**Use cases:**

- A button sound in a mobile app
- A notification in an online course
- A transition sound in a video
- A sound logo for your brand (3-5 seconds)

**Example prompts:**

```
# Notification sound
soft notification chime, professional app sound, 0.5 seconds

# Video transition
whoosh transition sound, clean and modern, 1 second

# Successful action
success confirmation sound, positive and light, 0.8 seconds

# Sound logo
brand logo sound, 3 seconds, rising tone, 
modern tech company feel, memorable
```

**Price:** sound effects use up credits on your ElevenLabs plan. The Free plan gives you 10,000 credits, without a commercial license (as of October 2026; current terms are on the [What's current](https://aimayak.com/en/now/) page); for projects you monetize, you need a paid plan.

## Practice

1. Create a Suno account (free):

Go to [suno.com](https://suno.com) → select Log in → pick a sign-in method, for example your Google account → you get free credits (50 a day on Free; downloads on the free plan are limited to a few trial files, and commercial use needs a paid plan)

2. Generate background music for YouTube with Claude's help:

```
Prompt for Claude:
Write 3 versions of a Suno prompt.
Purpose: background music for a YouTube channel about [your topic].
Prompt format: genre + BPM + mood + instruments + length.
No vocals.
```

3. Run each prompt in Suno and listen to the results (6 tracks = 3 prompts × 2 versions)

4. Have Claude write lyrics for a 15-second jingle for your brand:

```
Brand: [yours]
What it does: [one sentence]
Style: [what you want]
Write 4 lines + a 2-line chorus
```

5. Create a track in Suno with Custom Mode: paste in Claude's lyrics and pick a style

6. Go to ElevenLabs Sound Effects and create a transition sound for your videos:

```
# Try a few variations:
"cinematic whoosh, 1 second, clean"
"soft page turn sound, 0.5 seconds"
"subtle tech transition, 0.8 seconds"
```

**Check yourself:** your Suno library holds six background tracks and a jingle with words, you've picked the best background track and can say why, and in ElevenLabs you have a transition sound. All of this was made on free plans, so it's fine for learning and personal use, but not for commercial projects.

## Key takeaways

> Suno and similar services don't replace live musicians on complex projects, but they cover a large share of what someone who runs a business on content needs: background music, jingles, transitions. Fast and one of a kind, and on a paid plan with commercial rights, with a clear license.

> A good music prompt = genre + tempo in BPM + mood + instruments + length. Those five elements give you a predictable result, unlike "make something nice."

> In the music process, Claude is the lyricist and the prompt editor: it doesn't generate sound, but it helps you write a precise brief for the tools that do.
